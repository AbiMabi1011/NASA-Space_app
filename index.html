How FieldShift uses NASA data (Transparent Methodology)

We compare the selected season's NASA POWER rainfall totals with that season's median in the available decade. A season below 75% of that median is counted as "drier." The ranking uses seven 1–5 prototype traits: water efficiency, dry-condition fit, soil-building potential, market options, soil-texture fit, ground cover, and legume inclusion. Your priority sets the relative weights; water access adjusts the water-efficiency weight, and a higher frequency of drier seasons raises the dry-condition weight. We normalize the weighted average and multiply by 20 for a score out of 100. Yala (March–August) and Maha (September–February) are broad dry-zone planning windows; local crop calendars differ. NASA POWER meteorological variables are gridded, model-based climate data, not measurements from this field. The ranking is not a crop-yield or drought forecast.
